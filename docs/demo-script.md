# Ellis — ~80 second demo script

Two voices: a presenter (framing/voiceover) and the applicant (the person talking to Ellis). Ellis's
own lines are **representative, not literal** — the conversation engine is Claude-driven now, so a real
take will phrase things differently turn to turn. What matters for the demo is that the applicant's
side sounds like a real person, not a form-filler reading answers off a list — that's the actual point
being demonstrated.

---

**[0:00–0:08] PRESENTER** (voiceover or on camera)
> "This is Ellis — an AI that helps immigrants prep their citizenship paperwork, just by talking. Watch
> how natural this actually is."

**[0:08–0:12] ELLIS**
> "Hi, I'm Ellis. I'm not a lawyer, but I'll help get your citizenship application ready. Do you agree
> to continue?"

**[0:12–0:14] APPLICANT**
> "Yeah, let's do it."

**[0:14–0:18] ELLIS**
> "Great — your green card says you've been a resident since January 15th, 2022. Is that right?"

**[0:18–0:20] APPLICANT**
> "Yep, that's right."

**[0:20–0:25] ELLIS**
> "Perfect. Where are you living these days?"

**[0:25–0:29] APPLICANT**
> "I'm in Berkeley, California — zip's 94704."
>
> *(one sentence, three facts — Ellis records city, state and ZIP from it and moves straight on,
> doesn't ask the state separately)*

**[0:29–0:33] ELLIS**
> "Got it. And have you traveled outside the U.S. in the last five years?"

**[0:33–0:38] APPLICANT**
> "Um, I think once? I went to Mexico... honestly I'm not totally sure on the exact dates."
>
> *(deliberately hedged/messy — shows the system catching uncertainty instead of just accepting it)*

**[0:38–0:44] ELLIS**
> "No worries — we'll check that against your passport stamps, and come back to it if anything doesn't
> line up."

**[0:44–0:48] — CUT TO: clinic dashboard, background agent activity updating live**

**PRESENTER** (voiceover)
> "While they're talking, four AI agents are already working in the background — checking documents,
> catching conflicts, building the actual form."

**[0:48–0:54] — on screen: a flag appears** ("passport shows an entry with no matching trip on file"),
a deadline gets computed, a document gap gets added to the list — happening live, not after the call.

**[0:54–0:62] ELLIS**
> "One more thing — have you ever received a traffic ticket or citation?"

**APPLICANT**
> "Oh — yeah, actually. I got a speeding ticket a couple years ago."

**[0:62–0:68] ELLIS**
> "Thanks for telling me. I'm noting that for your clinic to look at — it's not a decision about your
> case, just something they'll review."

**[0:68–0:74] — CUT TO: the packet screen** — N-400 draft, evidence index, filing checklist, all
already generated.

**PRESENTER** (voiceover)
> "No forms. No checklists. Just a conversation — and a filing-ready packet on the other end."

**[0:74–0:80] — Ellis logo / tagline card.**

---

## Notes for whoever runs this live

- Keep the applicant's actual delivery conversational — a little overlapping, a little informal. The
  "Berkeley, California, zip's 94704" line and the hedged travel answer are the two beats that most
  directly prove the system isn't running a fixed script; don't smooth them into clean Q&A phrasing
  when performing this.
- If Ellis's real response differs from the line here, that's expected and fine — don't re-record to
  match the script, the whole point is that it's actually dynamic.
- The two on-screen cutaways (background agents, packet screen) are the parts that show the *backend*
  doing real work, not just a chatbot — don't cut those for time if the spoken parts run long; trim the
  presenter's framing lines instead.
